Hans Haacke, "News" 1969

One of the first electronic artworks to feature a live feed in the gallery, Haacke’s News (1969) consists of a real time printed newsfeed via a teletype machine. A pile of paper grows as it spills from the printer on the table onto the floor, and visitors are welcome to pick up portions to read. News does not analyze news content but simply delivers it, spewing out stories upon stories.

Hans Haacke, "News" 2008

News was to be re-exhibited at SFMOMA in 2018 for the Nothing Stable under Heaven show. I was asked to re-write the software as the 2008 version had become obsolete. The software programming language that the 2008 version of news was written in, called Lasso Professional was a relatively obscure proprietary programming language that would not run on modern operating systems. In this image, a part of the 2018 software is shown. In the rewrite we worked closely with the artists studio, which provided a set of parameters for migration, for example: a) printer should be placed on a simple table that does not draw attention to itself. b) Any printer that produces the news bulletins on paper as they come in, dot matrix or other. In case such paper print-outs can technically no longer be produced in the future, an LED or a comparable presentation could be considered. c) New sources, only in English, from all major centers around the world that affect the political events of the time. Inclusion of sources representing opposing sides of conflicts that have repercussions beyond their respective region. All continents should be represented. d) The 2008 computer program developed for "The Art of Participation: 1950 to Now" will most likely have to be adjusted, as technology develops.

Hans Haacke, "News" 2018

After the 2018 exhibition at SFMOMA the work went on loan to the Smithsonian American Art Museum. It was interesting because the registrar required that SAAM send a physical computer that I was to prepare and send back. Unfortunately the hardware failed. After some discussion, I was able to deliver the code electronically with instructions on how to install it and things were running on a new computer in 2 hours. This was interesting as these electronic mediums allow for different models of loans management and delivery, that perhaps differ from traditional loan processes

One of the projects I've been most proud of was the stewardship of the archives for graphic designer Susan Kare. Kare designed the first icons for the Macintosh operating system, including the garbage can and the happy face.

In 2016 she gifted a collection of 300 floppy disks to SFMOMA and MoMA. We spent a year collaborating on the restoration of these digital files and preparing them to become part of the respective museum's collections

Teiji Furuhashi's "Lovers", MoMA 2016

Lovers is an immersive, room-sized multimedia installation by Japanese artist Teiji Furuhashi (1960–1995). Life-sized images of the artist and other fellow members of the Kyoto-based artist collective Dumb Type are projected onto the walls of a darkened room from a tower of computer-controlled video and slide projectors at its center. The figures move like specters around the perimeter of the space, in a looped choreographic sequence made variable by a visitor-activated motion sensor, which intervenes to restart one of the projections when triggered.
